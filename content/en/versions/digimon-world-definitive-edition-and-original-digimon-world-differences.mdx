Digimon World Definitive Edition is not an official Bandai Namco release — it's a fan project currently in development, announced through the IAnime Studios YouTube channel and shared by community outlets like Digimon Fans Chile. Because it's still a work in progress, the differences listed below are what the project's creators have said the mod will include, not features from a finished product. If you loved the original Digimon World on PlayStation, this comparison matters: it's the clearest sign that the community is actively trying to modernize a cult classic that Bandai Namco has never remade.

## What Is Digimon World Definitive Edition?

The project is being developed by fans associated with IAnime Studios, with updates posted through the project's Discord server. It aims to be an enhanced version of the original Digimon World — the 1999 raising-sim RPG where you train a Digimon partner, recruit wild Digimon to File City, and explore the Digital World.

Since it's community-made and still in development, there's no confirmed release date. Fans who want to track progress are directed to the project's Discord for update posts.

## Confirmed Differences From the Original Digimon World

Based on the details shared via IAnime Studios and reported by Digimon Fans Chile, the Definitive Edition is planned to include the following changes:

- **Widescreen 16:9 display** — the original was built for 4:3 CRT televisions, so this is the most visible modernization.
- **New Digimon** — additional creatures beyond the original roster.
- **New evolution routes** — extra digivolution paths for raising your partner.
- **New attacks** — expanded move sets for battles.
- **New maps** — additional areas to explore beyond the original Digital World.
- **Three language options** — localization beyond the original release's language support.

The announcement also referenced "and more," suggesting the final scope may grow as development continues.

## What Stays the Same?

The project doesn't appear to be a remake from scratch — the framing as a "Definitive Edition" implies it builds on the original game's structure, maps, and core raising mechanics, then layers improvements on top. However, the available information doesn't detail exactly how the underlying gameplay (training, care mistakes, lifespan, recruitable Digimon) will be handled, so treat the core experience as unconfirmed until the team shares more.

## A Note of Caution for Fans

Because this is a fan project, it's worth tempering expectations. Fan modifications can be paused, redesigned, or cancelled, and the feature list above comes from the developers' own announcements rather than hands-on testing. Nothing here should be read as confirmation of a release date, platforms, or download availability. Still, the wishlist — widescreen support, new Digimon, new evolutions, and expanded maps — addresses many of the most common complaints about the aging original, which is exactly why the announcement generated buzz in the community.
